# Law, Actually — Format Bible

Full detail: [docs/PLAYBOOK.md](../../docs/PLAYBOOK.md), Channel 3.

## Positioning

Law, Actually explains what the law really does when ordinary life becomes
legally complicated. Not a claims channel, and deliberately not built
around personal injury lead flow. Coverage: criminal law, family law,
divorce, custody, consumer law, employment, property, contracts, police
powers, civil liability, inheritance, court procedure, notable outcomes,
counterintuitive rules.

## Separation from Unclaimed (binding)

Unclaimed asks whether there is money you may be entitled to recover.
Law, Actually asks what happens legally when a situation occurs. Subjects
may touch; the editorial question never does. State the differing question
in every backlog entry that could overlap.

## The legal review gate — without exception

**Nothing publishes without human legal review.** This is a liability
exposure, not a credibility one. The reviewer is named, the sign-off is
recorded in the episode's `review.md`, and cadence is capped by review
capacity, not production capacity.

## Legal and platform discipline (binding)

- General information only; no personalised advice; no guaranteed outcomes.
- Jurisdictional framing decided **before** research begins.
- State differences said **on screen**, not buried in a disclaimer.
- No exploiting active tragedies. No graphic imagery. Restrained
  thumbnails on violent or sensitive cases.
- Sponsors and commercial relationships disclosed clearly.
- The restraint in tone is also a monetisation decision: non-graphic,
  non-exploitative educational treatment keeps ad eligibility.

## Production notes specific to this channel

- One legal problem per episode. Real-world examples. Plain language.
- Research statutes, court rules, official guides, reported decisions.
- Source archive established for every published claim.
- Human fact-check sign-off AND legal review are both mandatory gates.
- Shorts come from the counterintuitive rule.
- Recurring series to build: "Why They Walked" and "What Happens If".
  Notable-case episodes only where the case explains a broader mechanism.
