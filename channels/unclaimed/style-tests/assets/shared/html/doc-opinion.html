Supreme Court of the United States

TYLER v. HENNEPIN COUNTY
on writ of certiorari
No.    Argued    Decided 2023

Opinion of the Court

The question presented is whether a county, having seized property to satisfy a debt of property tax, may retain the entire proceeds of the sale of that property, including the portion of those proceeds that exceeds the debt owed.

The record establishes that a debt of property tax in the amount of $15,000 was outstanding against a condominium the owner no longer occupied. The county seized the property and sold it for $40,000. The county retained the whole of that amount.

The county does not dispute that the surplus of $25,000 exceeded any obligation the owner bore to it. It contends only that state law extinguished the owner's interest in that surplus at the moment of forfeiture.

A taxpayer who loses her property to satisfy a debt of tax retains a property interest in the surplus. The county may keep what is owed to it. It may not keep more.

The county's retention of proceeds beyond the debt owed is therefore a taking of property without just compensation, and it is unconstitutional.

It is so ordered.
