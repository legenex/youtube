Unclaimed · style test

Five visual styles, one script

Episode 5, Tyler v. Hennepin County. Same narration, same voiceover file, same music bed, same 25 second runtime in all five. Only the grade, the type and the cutting change, so the comparison is fair. Every figure and hold below is measured from the finished file with ffmpeg scene detection at a threshold of 0.1, not estimated. Total generation spend across all five was 54 Higgsfield credits for a shared pool of 16 assets.

Style guide 1

Style 1 · Redacted Document

Look in one sentence

A real document fills the frame in near darkness, and exactly one line burns emerald while everything around it stays dim.

Palette

HexRole
#050505Ground. The paper is inverted, so the page body reads as near black.
#3AAE5CThe lit line. Emerald is reserved for the single fact currently landing, and for money. Nothing else is ever this colour.
#F5F0E3Second sign-off line only. Bone white closes the film.
#8A8578Caption grey under a figure. Says what the figure is.

Dim layer sits between 34 and 80 percent of the inverted page depending on the shot, so consecutive pages differ in tonal mass. Never let two adjacent shots sit at the same dim level.

Typography

The document supplies its own type: Times New Roman for the court filing, Arial for the county listing, Courier for the notice. Do not add display type except for a figure. Figures are Arial Black, 112 px on a 720 px frame (roughly one sixth of frame height), centred in the lower third on an opaque plate at #050505 so they never sit on the lit line. The caption under a figure is Arial Bold, 22 px, letter spaced 0.26 em, upper case.

Shot grammar

Three framings only, and they alternate:

  • Wide: the page fills the frame, one line lit.
  • Mid: a block of the page, one line lit.
  • Close: the lit line fills the frame, so emerald dominates.

Each shot is a slow push of 4 to 5 percent. Cuts are hard, with no transition. The rule that carries the style: never two lit lines at once, and never two consecutive shots from the same document. Cut wide page, close line, new document, and repeat.

Rhythm spec

About 48 shots per minute. Maximum static hold 2.5 seconds; measured 2.47. The only pause is the sign-off, which gets three beats over the last 3.5 seconds so it still moves while the voice lands.

Asset recipe

100 percent document facsimile, screenshotted from hand built HTML at 2x. Zero stock, zero generated imagery, zero screen recording. Type is rendered as transparent PNG. This is the cheapest style in the set by a wide margin.

Cost per 10 minute episode

Generation calls: 0. Stock clips: 0. Documents: 6 to 10 HTML facsimiles, about 3 hours to build and tag. Editing: about 4 hours, most of it choosing which line lights on which word. Total about 7 hours, no spend.

Three rules that must never be broken

  1. One lit line at a time. Two is an instant fail.
  2. Emerald is only ever the lit fact or money. Never decoration.
  3. Every figure carries a grey caption saying what it is. A number alone on a document invites the viewer to misread which number it is.

    4. What this style is bad at

    Anything physical. It cannot show a house, a person, a place or a feeling. It has no warmth and no faces, so a 10 minute episode in this style alone will feel airless. Use it for the evidence spine and cut away to something with air.

Style guide 2

Style 2 · Kinetic Type Over Footage

Look in one sentence

Bold condensed type slams onto the beat while heavily graded footage runs underneath as a texture, never as the subject.

Palette

HexRole
#0D0D0DGround. The grade crushes all footage toward this.
#F5F0E3Body type. Every statement line.
#3AAE5CMoney and the sign-off payoff. Emerald means a dollar figure or the promise.
#D8B26AWarm tan for figures that are not the payoff, so 15,000 and 40,000 read differently from 25,000.
#C0392BThe county. Used once, as a block behind "Hennepin County".

Footage is graded to saturation 0.14, contrast 1.22, brightness minus 0.22. It should be barely legible as a place.

Typography

Impact throughout, upper case, no exceptions. Statement lines 56 to 66 px on a 720 px frame. Payoff words 96 to 138 px, about one fifth of frame height. Figures 168 to 190 px, the largest thing that ever appears. Type is centred or flush left at 70 px, never flush right. Letter spacing 0.01 to 0.08 em, opened up as size drops. Every line carries a soft black shadow so it holds over any footage.

Shot grammar

A shot is one graded plate with one or two type lines. Lines arrive staggered, about 0.22 s apart, each dropping in over three frames from 20 px above its rest position, then locking hard. Colour blocks wipe across in 0.20 s on a new statement. Figures resolve out of redaction blocks: the number arrives as $██,███, then digits fill left to right, then it locks. That reveal invents no intermediate figure, which matters on this channel. Cuts are hard on the voice.

Rhythm spec

About 41 shots per minute. Maximum static hold 2.5 seconds; measured 1.90. Pauses sit on the two shortest sentences, "Not the debt" and "All of it", which get their own plates so the silence is visible.

Asset recipe

About 82 percent generated stills, 18 percent generated clips, zero document, zero screen recording. Any stock library would substitute directly for the generated footage, since the footage only needs to read as a dark texture.

Cost per 10 minute episode

Generation calls: 0 if stock is available, since this style does not need specific imagery. Otherwise about 20 stills and 6 clips. Stock clips: 20 to 30. Editing: about 6 hours, most of it timing type to the voice track.

Three rules that must never be broken

  1. Type is the subject. If the footage is competing, grade it down further.
  2. Emerald only for money and the sign-off. Tan for supporting figures.
  3. Never render a figure that is not in the script, not even for a frame of a count up. Use the redaction reveal instead.

    4. What this style is bad at

    Nuance and sequence. It states, it does not explain, so it cannot carry a process, a chronology or a caveat. It also gets tiring: past about 90 seconds the slams stop registering.

Style guide 3

Style 3 · Annotated Screen

Look in one sentence

A real government page sits on screen in its own browser greys while a hand marks it up in emerald, live, as the narration reaches each fact.

Palette

HexRole
#FFFFFF and #EEF0F4The page. Untouched browser white and chrome grey. Never graded.
#24365EThe masthead navy of the official site. Comes from the page, not from us.
#3AAE5CEvery annotation: circles, arrows, underlines, label chips. The only colour we add.
#07130BType inside an emerald chip.
#8D2013Comes from the page only, as the status column. Never used as an annotation.

The page must look like a real page. Resist the urge to grade or darken it.

Typography

The page supplies its own type. We add only Inter SemiBold label chips, 32 to 42 px on a 720 px frame, sentence case, on a solid emerald plate with 12 by 20 px padding. Chips are short: two to five words. They name what the mark means, they do not restate the narration.

Shot grammar

A shot is a framing of the page plus one annotation that draws itself on. The mark is a pre rendered hand drawn stroke revealed left to right over 0.32 s by an animated alpha mask, so it reads as being drawn rather than appearing. An oversized cursor moves between targets on the shots where a click or a field matters. Framing pushes 3 to 15 percent through the shot, and pushes hardest on the payoff, where the circled area fills the frame. Cuts are hard, and each cut either changes the framing scale or changes the page.

Rhythm spec

About 48 shots per minute. Maximum static hold 2.5 seconds; measured 1.93. Pauses go where the viewer needs to read: the alert box about fees, and the circled surplus figure, both get the longest holds in the film.

Asset recipe

100 percent screen: browser page facsimiles and document facsimiles built in HTML and screenshotted at 2x. Annotations are seven reusable transparent PNGs. Zero stock, zero generated imagery. Like style 1, this costs nothing per shot.

Cost per 10 minute episode

Generation calls: 0. Stock clips: 0. Build the page facsimiles once, about 4 hours, then reuse them across the whole series. Annotation set is built once and never rebuilt. Editing: about 5 hours, mostly aligning marks to targets.

Three rules that must never be broken

  1. The page is never graded, tinted or darkened. Its credibility is the style.
  2. Emerald is ours, everything else belongs to the page. Never annotate in the page's own red.
  3. Never annotate a field that is not really there. If the page does not show it, do not draw an arrow to it.

    4. What this style is bad at

    Emotion and scale. It is a screen, so it cannot make you feel what losing a house is like, and it cannot show a country. It is also fragile: if the facsimile looks fake, the whole style collapses.

Style guide 4

Style 4 · High Speed Cut

Look in one sentence

Something changes every half second in a serious register, so the speed itself becomes the argument that this is urgent.

Palette

HexRole
#0D0D0DGround and the flash colour for neutral beats.
#E8E8E8Mono type. Every statement.
#3AAE5CMoney, and the emerald two frame flash that punctuates a figure.
#C0392BThe county. One red, used for the actor who took the money, and for one flash.

Footage grade is lighter than style 2: saturation 0.34, contrast 1.28. The images need to be readable in half a second, so they cannot be crushed.

Typography

Helvetica Neue Black Condensed, upper case, centred. Statements 52 to 98 px on a 720 px frame. Payoff words 120 to 200 px. One line per shot, never two: at this speed a second line cannot be read. Letter spacing 0.01 to 0.14 em. Text sits at 250 to 330 px from the top, so it never fights the lower edge.

Shot grammar

A shot is one image, one zoom punch of 8 to 22 percent, and at most one line of type. Every cut is hard. Two frame flashes of solid colour sit between beats that need separation, emerald after a figure, red after the county, ink after a statement. Documents appear as inverted crops so they cut cleanly against photographic frames. The grammar is relentless but not random: images alternate photographic, document, photographic, so the eye keeps re-anchoring.

Rhythm spec

About 159 shots per minute. Maximum static hold 1 second; measured 0.70. Average interval 0.38 s. There are no pauses. The only concession is the final line, which gets two shots instead of one so the sign-off lands rather than flies past.

Asset recipe

About 49 percent generated stills, 9 percent generated clips, 21 percent document crops, 21 percent solid colour flashes. The flashes and documents cost nothing, which is why this style is far cheaper than its shot count suggests.

Cost per 10 minute episode

This is the expensive one. At 159 shots per minute a 10 minute episode needs roughly 1,600 distinct visual beats. Reuse is mandatory: budget about 60 stills and 12 clips, then cut each asset 8 to 12 ways with different crops, zooms and type. Generation calls: about 72. Stock clips: 40 or more if available. Editing: about 14 hours.

Three rules that must never be broken

  1. No jokes, no memes, no irony. The pacing is borrowed from tech explainers, the register is not.
  2. One line of type per shot.
  3. No flash longer than two frames, and never a flash on the sign-off.

    4. What this style is bad at

    Anything that needs to be read or understood. It cannot carry a document, a process, a caveat or a number the viewer has to check. It also cannot breathe, so it is exhausting at length and it fights the calm plain voice this channel uses. Best as a 20 second opening, not a whole episode.

Style guide 5

Style 5 · Case File

Look in one sentence

A manila folder opens on a dark desk under one warm side light, and the evidence accumulates: documents taped down, photographs at slight angles, typewriter labels stamped on, red thread connecting two facts.

Palette

HexRole
#17110A to #0B0806The desk. A warm near black, lit from upper left only.
#CFA863The folder. Manila, the one large warm mass in frame.
#EFE9DAPaper. Documents and typewriter labels.
#3AAE5CEmerald label, used once, for the surplus that was kept.
#8D2013Red thread, pins and the vote stamp. Connection and finding.

Typography

Courier New Bold throughout, because everything is a typed label. Labels 30 px, figure labels 54 px on a 720 px frame, upper case, letter spaced 0.14 to 0.20 em, on paper plates with a hard drop shadow. Labels are rotated 1 to 3 degrees. Nothing is ever perfectly straight in this style.

Shot grammar

A shot is a framing of the board in one state of accumulation. Between shots the board gains an item, so cutting back wider reveals what arrived. Every shot carries continuous slow motion: a 4 to 32 percent zoom plus a lateral drift of up to 6 percent of frame, with the direction reversed on each cut so the film never settles. Items sit at 1 to 3 degrees of rotation, held by tape or a clip. Red thread arrives late, at the ruling, and connects the notice to the opinion.

Rhythm spec

About 38 shots per minute, the slowest in the set. Maximum static hold 3 seconds; measured 2.00. There is never a frozen frame, even in a hold. Pauses go on the two beats where an item lands: the surplus label and the vote stamp.

Asset recipe

100 percent composite: the board is built in HTML from 3 document facsimiles and 3 generated stills, then screenshotted at nine states of accumulation. No shot is a bare stock clip. Generated imagery is only ever a photograph pinned to the board, never a full frame.

Cost per 10 minute episode

Generation calls: about 12 stills, since each one becomes a pinned photograph and can be reused across states. Stock clips: 0. Board build: about 5 hours for the first episode, then 1 hour per episode to restate. Editing: about 5 hours. Cheap after the first build, and the most reusable style in the set.

Three rules that must never be broken

  1. Never freeze. If a shot has no drift it has failed, no matter how long it is.
  2. One emerald item per board. The surplus is the only thing that gets it.
  3. Nothing is straight and nothing is centred. The moment the board looks designed rather than assembled, the style dies.

    4. What this style is bad at

    Speed and clarity of a single number. It is a slow accumulating look, so it cannot land a fast statistic, and a viewer on a phone will struggle to read any document pinned to the board. Treat pinned documents as texture, and put any figure the viewer must read on a label.
